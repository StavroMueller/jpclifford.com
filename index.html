02 · Atelier Actara

A platform that runs fashion shows

Atelier Actara carries a show from casting to the door. Producers build the lineup and walk order, assign looks, plan the venue, and run the guest list and check-in. Agency bookers manage a roster across many shows. Models, crew, and media confirm their own bookings and read their own call times. Agencies, brands, and venues work the same show without seeing each other's data. I made the first commit in December 2022, built it alongside full-time roles for three and a half years, and in June 2026 it became its own company with me as founder and principal engineer.

Who uses it

The customers are production houses, independent producers, modeling agencies, and fashion week organizers. Each show's public page, with its talent profiles and producer-confirmed credits, stays up free as a record of who worked it. Pricing has a per-seat subscription for producers, bookers, and crew, a per-show tier for one-off events, and an enterprise tier for large agencies and fashion weeks.

How it is built

The back end is an Elixir and Phoenix umbrella of twenty apps, with Absinthe GraphQL in front of it. Shows, organizations, participants, permissions, venues, runways, looks, guests, messaging, billing, delivery, and imports each live in their own app. Oban runs background jobs, Stripe handles payments, and Postgres holds the data.

Four clients share that one schema. The web app is React 19 with Apollo Client and MUI. The iOS app is native Swift, about twenty thousand lines, for models, crew, and media. The Android app is Jetpack Compose with Apollo Kotlin and Hilt. An admin CLI talks to the same API, so validation and business rules exist once. A separate superadmin back office runs on Fly.io.

How I work on it

I am the only engineer, and AI-assisted development is the documented standard. The repository carries written engineering conventions, a product document that governs every word on screen, agent skill definitions, and verification gates that run before a change ships, including a script that fails copy on known machine-writing tells. A set of agents playing CEO, PM, CTO, developer, QA, and COO run sprint loops against the repository and coordinate through shared files, and I review what they produce.

What else I own

I set the pricing, wrote the financial plan and the investor materials, and designed the brand and the design system the product ships with. I also produce shows through the platform, which is where most of the feature requests come from.

04 · At sea

Crew aboard Elissa

Elissa is a three-masted iron barque built in Aberdeen in 1877 and sailed today by volunteer crew out of Galveston. I stand watches, work aloft, and handle sail on day sails in the Gulf.

The 2026 passage

In June 2026 she sailed from the Gulf up the Atlantic coast to New York, and I was aboard. The passage rounded Cape Hatteras, went up Chesapeake Bay to Annapolis, through the C&D Canal, and out Delaware Bay into the Atlantic for the run up the New Jersey coast.

onlytallships.com

I kept the ship's log for that passage underway and published it at onlytallships.com. The site is built with Astro and has a Leaflet chart of every geotagged entry, an RSS feed, a guestbook, and a small browser editor for writing entries from the ship.

2023 to 2026 · JavaScript · three.js

Rod Tips Only

Rod Tips Only puts five fishing rods on a dock in Louisiana and waits for a bite. I model each rod as a tapered cantilever beam, a chain of rigid links joined by torsion springs and solved with XPBD, so the bend under a fish matches a real rod. It runs as a PWA and has a replay system, catch cards, and a depression meter.
